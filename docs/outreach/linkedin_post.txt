Building a next-gen SSD: Ferroelectric Tunnel Junction (FTJ) memory, simulated before tape-out.

I built a C++20 storage controller that simulates FTJ non-volatile memory — byte-addressable, ~8ns access, >10^10 write endurance (vs ~3k cycles for 3D NAND). It runs as a real Windows virtual disk (WinFSP) and benchmarks at 205K IOPS, 6.8us median latency.

Why it matters: AI workloads (KV-cache, vector DB, WAL) kill NAND in months. FTJ on mature 28nm foundries fixes the durability crisis — and bypasses the sub-7nm monopoly inflating hardware prices.

Status:
