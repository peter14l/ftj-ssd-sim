We have verified the physics and controller architecture in simulation. Now, it is time to build it into physical silicon hardware.

For the past several months, I have been working on solving one of the costliest physical bottlenecks in modern AI infrastructure: The Flash Storage Wear-Out Crisis.

High-throughput AI workloads (LLM KV-caching, vector retrieval, real-time database indexing) are punishing enterprise 3D NAND SSDs. Because legacy flash cannot overwrite bytes without erasing multi-megabyte blocks (incurring severe 3ms latency jitter and rapid cell wear-out), enterprise drives face high write amplification and burn out prematurely.

To address this, I designed and verified an end-to-end controller architecture for Ferroelectric Tunnel Junction (FTJ) Memory:

What Has Been Validated (TRL 3 / Early TRL 4):
1. In-Silico Physics Modeling: C++20 cycle-accurate engine simulating quantum tunneling electroresistance (TER), Merz’s Law switching dynamics, and crossbar IR-drop constraints under thermal stress.
2. Hardware RTL: Synthesizable Verilog modules for lock-free circular NVMe queue pairs and SECDED Hamming (72/64) error correction.
3. System-Level Verification: Integrated with a live Windows virtual filesystem driver (WinFSP), achieving sub-microsecond latency and >400x write-speed improvements over 3D NAND under concurrent stress workloads.
4. The 28nm Economic Strategy: Designed specifically for mature 28nm planar CMOS foundries using CMOS-compatible doped Hafnium Oxide (HfO2) thin films — bypassing prohibitive $50M+ sub-7nm mask costs.
